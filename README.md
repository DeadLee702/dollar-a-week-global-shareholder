🌍 THE 10-CENT INITIATIVE

A Global Initiative

The 10-Cent Initiative begins with an idea simple enough for anyone, anywhere to understand:

Participate at a level you can afford.

The starting point is the equivalent of 10 U.S. cents in your local currency.

It is not a required weekly payment. It is not a subscription. It is not a debt.

It is an invitation to participate.

A person may contribute 10 cents, more than 10 cents, or less when circumstances require it.

The important thing is participation.

---

🌎 Everyone, Everywhere

This initiative is designed for people everywhere.

It does not matter:

- What country you live in
- What language you speak
- What culture you come from
- Whether you live in a major city, a small town, or a remote village
- Whether you have a smartphone, feature phone, or no phone
- Whether you have a bank account, digital wallet, or only access to cash
- Whether you can contribute 10 cents, 25 cents, $1, $10, or more

The initiative begins with a simple amount because simple ideas are easy to understand.

The equivalent of 10 cents can mean something different in different parts of the world.

The app therefore presents contributions in the participant's local currency and language.

---

📍 Start Where You Are

The application begins by establishing where the participant is.

With permission, the app uses live location information to identify the participant's geographic area as accurately as available data allows.

The purpose is not to assume who someone is.

The purpose is to understand where they are.

The app can then present the languages commonly used in that area.

The participant chooses their language.

If their language or local dialect is not listed, they can enter it themselves.

The person chooses the language.

---

🗣️ Speak to People in Terms They Understand

Translation alone is not enough.

The initiative is global, which means information must be understandable to people with different languages, cultures, currencies, and everyday experiences.

The application is designed to present the same underlying information in terms people can understand.

The facts remain the facts.

The way those facts are communicated can adapt to the person.

---

💰 The Power of 10 Cents

The starting point is intentionally small.

Imagine 2.5 billion people participating at the equivalent of 10 cents each:

$250 million per week.

Now imagine participation growing.

At the same 2.5 billion participants:

$0.10 → $250 million/week

$1 → $2.5 billion/week

$10 → $25 billion/week

The larger the participation and the larger the voluntary contribution, the greater the collective capital becomes.

The initiative does not need to begin at its eventual scale.

It needs to begin.

## Universal Micro-Contribution Access

Make participation at the equivalent of 10 U.S. cents effortless regardless of:

- Device
- Technical literacy
- Internet access
- Banking access
- Location
- Language
- Dialect
- Payment method

The application must meet people where they already handle money rather than requiring everyone to obtain a smartphone, bank account, cryptocurrency wallet, or new financial service.

### Feature-Phone / USSD Participation

Support participation through appropriate local mobile networks and USSD systems where available.

Example:

*789*10#

The exact shortcode is determined by the participating telecommunications/payment provider.

Where technically and legally supported, the participant can authorize the local equivalent of 10 cents to be deducted from prepaid mobile credit or another supported mobile balance.

No smartphone required.

No bank account required.

### Local Payment Systems

Integrate with dominant payment systems in each supported region.

Examples may include:

- M-Pesa
- UPI
- Pix
- Cash App
- Venmo
- Other locally dominant payment systems

Provide a one-tap action that opens the participant's supported payment application with the correct local-currency amount prepared for confirmation.

Never assume one payment system works everywhere.

### Voice Contributions

Integrate Cultural Rosetta for natural-language contribution requests.

A participant can speak naturally in their own language or dialect.

Example:

"Put in my 10 cents for the week."

The system:

1. Understands the request.
2. Determines the participant's intended contribution.
3. Converts the amount to the appropriate local currency.
4. Clearly confirms the amount.
5. Requests the required payment authorization.
6. Executes the transaction through the supported payment provider.
7. Records the verified contribution.

Voice interpretation must never independently authorize a financial transaction without the required participant confirmation and payment-provider authorization.

### Cash Participation

Support cash-dominant communities.

A participant can provide the local equivalent of the contribution to an authorized participating merchant, kiosk, community location, or other approved collection point.

The authorized location can:

- Scan a reusable participation card
- Scan a participant code
- Generate a transaction code
- Print a paper receipt/code
- Record the contribution through an approved offline process

The contribution must enter the same transparent ledger after verification.

Cash collection must include appropriate fraud prevention, reconciliation, authorization, and audit controls.

### Automatic Round-Ups

For participants who choose to use digital payments, provide an optional round-up mechanism.

Example:

Purchase:
$4.63

Round-up:
$0.37

The participant can accumulate spare change until it reaches their chosen contribution amount.

Round-ups are completely optional.

The participant controls:
- Whether round-ups are enabled
- The contribution target
- The funding account
- The ability to pause or cancel the feature

### Offline Participation

Support regions with unreliable connectivity.

Where technically and cryptographically appropriate, allow an authorized device to create a locally signed pending contribution record.

The record can contain:

- Participant identifier
- Contribution amount
- Local currency
- Timestamp
- Device/session information
- Cryptographic signature
- Pending status

When connectivity becomes available, the device synchronizes with the central system.

The ledger must distinguish:

PENDING OFFLINE RECORD

from:

VERIFIED CONTRIBUTION

No offline record should be treated as final financial settlement until the required verification and reconciliation process succeeds.

Support synchronization through available connectivity, including appropriate local network or mesh infrastructure where implemented.

## Closed-Loop Participation Card Network

Build an optional closed-loop stored-value participation network that allows people to participate without requiring Visa, Mastercard, traditional card acquiring, or a conventional bank account.

The card is not intended to function as a traditional credit card.

It acts primarily as a participant identifier and secure access mechanism connected to the initiative's internal ledger.

### Physical Participation Cards

Support extremely low-cost physical cards or participation credentials.

Possible formats:

- NFC card
- QR card
- Paper QR credential
- Durable reusable card
- Other low-cost secure physical media

Each credential contains or represents a unique identifier.

Where appropriate, use hardware-backed cryptographic keys rather than storing sensitive financial information directly on the card.

The card itself does not need to contain the participant's entire balance.

The secure ledger remains the authoritative record.

### Community Agent Kiosks

Allow authorized local agents to operate the system using inexpensive Android devices.

Potential agents include:

- Local merchants
- Community centers
- Authorized kiosks
- Regional organizations
- Mobile field representatives
- Other approved participation locations

A participant can provide local physical currency to an authorized agent.

The agent:

1. Identifies the participant.
2. Records the amount received.
3. Taps or scans the participant credential.
4. Creates the transaction.
5. Provides a receipt or transaction confirmation.
6. Synchronizes the transaction with the central ledger when connectivity is available.

### Closed-Loop Ledger

Once value has been legitimately deposited and credited, internal participant-to-initiative transactions can occur entirely within the initiative's own ledger.

This can eliminate external card-network interchange and payment-processing fees for transactions that never leave the closed-loop system.

The architecture must distinguish:

EXTERNAL MONEY ENTERING THE SYSTEM

from:

INTERNAL LEDGER TRANSFERS

The initiative must still account for legitimate costs associated with:

- Card production
- Agent operations
- Cash handling
- Connectivity
- Equipment
- Security
- Compliance
- Reconciliation
- Infrastructure

"Zero-fee execution" means eliminating unnecessary external transaction-network fees where technically possible, not claiming that operating the network has no costs.

### Offline Transactions

Support authorized offline participation where connectivity is unavailable.

The participant credential and authorized agent terminal can establish a cryptographically authenticated transaction.

The terminal records:

- Participant identifier
- Transaction amount
- Local currency
- Timestamp
- Transaction nonce
- Device identifier
- Cryptographic signature
- Transaction status

The transaction remains:

PENDING

until successfully synchronized and verified.

Once connectivity returns, the terminal synchronizes the transaction with the global ledger.

The system must prevent:

- Duplicate transactions
- Replay attacks
- Unauthorized balance creation
- Double spending
- Forged credentials
- Unauthorized agent activity

### Z-12 Verification

Use the existing Z-12 system as the deterministic security and verification layer.

Z-12 verifies the transaction according to the established protocol.

The application does not recreate Z-12.

It connects to the existing Z-12 infrastructure.

Transaction flow:

PARTICIPANT
→
CARD / QR CREDENTIAL
→
AUTHORIZED TERMINAL
→
LOCAL TRANSACTION
→
Z-12 VERIFICATION
→
GLOBAL LEDGER
→
TRANSPARENT RECORD

### Card Issuance

Potential distribution locations:

- Community hubs
- Schools

- Community organizations

- Authorized field representatives

Each credential must have a unique identity.

Lost or compromised credentials must be capable of being revoked and replaced.

### Top-Up

Participants can add value through authorized local agents.

Example:

Participant provides the local equivalent of $10.

Agent verifies the transaction.

Participant's internal stored value is credited.

The participant can then use the stored value for voluntary contributions according to the initiative's rules.

Every verified transaction becomes part of the initiative's transparent accounting system.

## Existing Technology Foundation

The 10-Cent Initiative is built on technology that already exists.

### Z-12

Z-12 provides deterministic verification, enforcement, and continuous compliance.

It is designed to ensure that critical protocol rules are not merely recommendations. They can be verified and enforced by the system.

### Space-Bound AI

Space-Bound AI provides the AI infrastructure for controlled, auditable AI operations.

It is designed to operate within defined boundaries rather than allowing an AI system to become an uncontrolled authority.

### Cultural Rosetta

Cultural Rosetta provides the foundation for communicating across languages, cultures, regions, and local expressions.

The objective is not simply to translate words.

The objective is to make information understandable to the person receiving it, including local language and cultural context.

### Together

These systems provide three important foundations for the initiative:

Z-12 → VERIFY

Space-Bound AI → OPERATE

Cultural Rosetta → COMMUNICATE

The 10-Cent Initiative brings these capabilities together around a global system designed to be understandable, transparent, verifiable, and accessible to people wherever they are.
